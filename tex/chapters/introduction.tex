Before we continue on with contamination, its prudent to note that water in its natural form is never \enquote{\texttt{pure}} in the way that we might think. It always contains some level of impurities, whether they are natural (like minerals) or anthropogenic (like pollutants from human activity). \subsection{Wastewater}
\subsection{Definition of Wastewater}
Wastewater is broadly defined as any water that has been used and is no longer suitable for its original purpose. It includes water contaminated with pollutants, chemicals, or microorganisms, rendering it unsafe for human consumption or environmental discharge without treatment. As defined by textbf{UN‑ESCWA}:

\enquote{Water which is of no further immediate value to the purpose for which it was used or in the pursuit of which it was produced because of its quality, quantity or time of occurrence. However, wastewater from one user can be a potential supply to another user elsewhere; the spent or used water from homes, communities, farms and businesses that contains enough harmful material to damage the water’s quality.}~\cite{unescwaWastewaterDefinition}

In an average household, water is supplied from a central source, such as a municipal water supply or a borehole. This water is used for various purposes, including drinking, cooking, bathing, and cleaning. After use, this water becomes \enquote{wastewater}—a term that encompasses all the used water that is no longer suitable for its original purpose.

According to Bclear Wastewater Solutions, wastewater can be classified into several categories, including domestic, industrial, agricultural, stormwater, hospital, mining, food processing, construction ande demolition, septic tank effluent, greywater, and landfill leachate~\cite{bclearWastewaterTypes}.
